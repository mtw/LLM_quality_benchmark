Text:
RNA molecules adopt ensembles of alternative conformations that influence translation, splicing, localization, and decay. Yet most computational methods predict a single minimum free-energy structure and therefore provide only a limited view of regulatory potential. Here, we present a workflow that combines thermodynamic ensemble prediction, local accessibility profiling, and sequence perturbation analysis to characterize structure-function relationships in messenger RNA untranslated regions. We applied the workflow to a benchmark set of synthetic and endogenous 5' untranslated regions with experimentally measured translation outputs. Ensemble-derived accessibility near the start codon correlated more strongly with translation than minimum free-energy structural features alone. In contrast, global GC content showed inconsistent association with expression across constructs. Sequence perturbation analyses further indicated that multiple sequence variants can preserve similar accessibility profiles despite differing substantially in minimum free-energy structure. The study was limited to in vitro reporter assays in two cell lines and did not test long-term RNA stability, innate immune sensing, or in vivo delivery. Taken together, the results support the view that ensemble-level structural properties provide useful descriptors for engineering translation-relevant RNA elements.
