Text:
Exoribonuclease-resistant RNAs (xrRNAs) are structured RNA elements that block processive 5'->3' degradation by XRN family nucleases. Previous work in several flaviviruses has shown that xrRNA function depends on a ring-like fold in which the 5' end is threaded through a topologically constrained architecture stabilized by pseudoknots and tertiary contacts. In the present study, we asked to what extent mechanical resistance can be engineered without preserving natural sequence ancestry. We first generated a systematic set of pseudoknot perturbation variants derived from a reference viral xrRNA and assayed their resistance to XRN1 degradation in vitro. We then combined symbolic structural constraints, length-correlation rules, and three-dimensional modeling to design synthetic candidate xrRNAs. Candidate molecules were filtered using molecular simulations intended to enrich for mechanically robust folds. Several synthetic constructs retained XRN1 resistance, including one de novo sequence with no meaningful primary-sequence similarity to known xrRNAs under the criteria used here. However, the study did not address ligand responsiveness, cellular behavior, or fitness consequences in a viral genome context. The results therefore support a topology-centered view of xrRNA design, while leaving broader functional deployment unresolved.
